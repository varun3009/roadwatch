A

Distance from a single camera

The selected ground point is undistorted using the camera intrinsics, then its ray is rotated using pitch, roll, and yaw. Intersecting that ray with a locally flat road gives a metric forward and lateral distance.

pixel → camera ray → vehicle frame → road intersection

Rays above the horizon, rays too close to the horizon, and positions outside the configured range are rejected. The result is an estimate, not a depth sensor measurement.

B

Coordinates that follow the vehicle

The camera offset from the GNSS antenna is added in the vehicle frame. Vehicle heading rotates forward and lateral offsets into north and east; these are converted to latitude and longitude.

GNSS position + heading + camera offset + hazard offset

GPS and heading are both required. Missing or stale fixes leave coordinates unavailable. GPS accuracy and the camera calibration limit hazard location accuracy.

C

Time alignment & reference matching

Dataset video time plus the configurable sync offset selects the corresponding GPS pose. References are compared by compatible class, physical separation, and time when timing is available.

video time + sync offset → pose → candidate reference

A nearby match is a candidate for review. Event-time vehicle positions can be several metres from the actual hazard. Use surveyed coordinates when available.

D

Stable observations, clear limits

Tracking and nearby repeated observations are combined into unique hazard records. Each record retains its observation count and match result. The live map uses these records while the camera view shows current detections.

frames → detections → repeated observations → unique hazards

The model only recognizes classes in its training. Cracks and other hazards require trained labels. Validate on your own route and lighting before relying on estimates.

Designed to change with your setup

Swap the model, camera, mounting geometry, source, or spreadsheet columns through the interface. Configuration is saved locally by the server.
